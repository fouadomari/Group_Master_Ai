🤔 Why this matters

Welcome to the last phase of PyPath: NLP, short for Natural Language Processing. "Natural language" means the language people write and speak, like English. NLP is how computers work with text: spam filters, search engines, translation apps, chatbots and the tools that read thousands of product reviews to tell a shop what customers think.

So far all our data was numbers in neat tables. Text is messier. To a computer, "Great", "great" and "great!!!" are three completely different things. Cleaning text is like washing vegetables before cooking: not exciting, but every recipe starts with it. Over the next four days you'll go from raw text to a program that reads reviews and decides whether they are happy or unhappy.

✂️ Step 2: remove punctuation

Python's built-in string module has a ready-made text called string.punctuation holding every punctuation mark. Together with two string tools we can delete them all at once:

  • str.maketrans("", "", string.punctuation) builds a translation table: a set of instructions saying "replace nothing with nothing, and delete these characters".
  • text.translate(table) follows those instructions on a piece of text.

🧩 Step 3: split into tokens

A token is one small piece of text, usually one word. Splitting text into tokens is called tokenising. The simplest way is .split(), which cuts the text wherever there are spaces and gives back a list:

Shift + Enter

Removing all punctuation has a downside: the apostrophe in i'd disappears and you get id, which is a different word. A more careful tool is a regular expression (from the re module): a tiny pattern language for finding text. re.findall(pattern, text) returns every piece of the text that matches the pattern.

Pattern pieceMeans
[a-z']one character that is a letter from a to z, or an apostrophe
+"one or more of the thing before me, in a row"
[a-z']+a run of letters (and apostrophes): a word!
[^a-z' ]the ^ means NOT: any character that is not a letter, apostrophe or space

The r before the quotes makes a raw string, which keeps backslashes exactly as typed. Patterns often contain backslashes, so programmers always write patterns as raw strings.

Shift + Enter

Stop words are very common words that carry little meaning on their own: the, a, is, and, it, to… Removing them leaves the words that matter. NLTK has a ready-made list, but it is another download, so we write our own short list.

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Calling a string method on a list: reviews.lower() → AttributeError. Loop over the list instead.
  • Forgetting to lowercase, so Great and great are counted separately.
  • Removing every punctuation mark, which turns i'd into id. Choose your cleaning on purpose.
  • Dropping not as a stop word when the meaning matters.
  • Expecting stems to be real words. batteri is fine: it only has to match.

✅ Recap

  • A document is one text; a corpus is a collection of them.
  • Cleaning: .lower(), then remove punctuation with str.maketrans + .translate or re.sub.
  • Tokens are the pieces of text: .split(), re.findall(r"[a-z']+", text) or NLTK's wordpunct_tokenize.
  • Stop words are common words with little meaning; filter them with a set.
  • Stemming (PorterStemmer) cuts words to a shared root.
  • Counter(list).most_common(n) finds the most frequent words.
