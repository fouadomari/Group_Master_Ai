🤔 Why this matters

Look at twelve monthly temperatures in a table and you see… twelve numbers. Draw them as a line and you instantly see summer rise and winter fall. Our eyes are much better at shapes than at columns of digits.

Charts are how data scientists explore data (spotting trends, odd values and patterns) and how they explain results to everyone else. Tomorrow's lesson, exploring a whole dataset, leans on today's charts. The tool we use is Matplotlib, the most widely used charting library in Python. pandas and many other libraries draw their charts with it behind the scenes.

Remember: the code boxes on this page share their variables, like pages of one notebook. Run them from top to bottom. The first box loads Matplotlib, which can take a few seconds. Charts appear as pictures under each box.

Matplotlib's charting tools live in matplotlib.pyplot, which everyone imports with the short name plt (like np and pd). plt.plot(x, y) draws a line through the points: the first list goes along the bottom (the x-axis), the second goes up the side (the y-axis). The temperatures are made up:

🏷️ Titles, labels and style

A chart without labels is a riddle. Always add a title and axis labels (with units!). You can also choose the size of the chart in inches with plt.figure(figsize=(width, height)), a colour, and a marker, a small symbol on each data point ("o" is a dot, "s" a square, "^" a triangle):

📶 Histograms: how values are spread

A histogram answers "which values are common and which are rare?". It cuts the range of values into equal slices called bins and draws one bar per bin, as tall as the number of values that fall into it. Let's make 150 made-up adult heights with NumPy's random generator (Day 9). The seed 42 makes the "random" numbers the same every time:

Shift + Enter

The typical "hill" shape: lots of people in the middle, few very short or very tall ones. Try bins=4 and bins=40: too few bins hide the shape, too many make it spiky. Somewhere between 10 and 30 is usually a good start.

⚫ Scatter plots: do two things go together?

A scatter plot draws one dot per person (or shop, or day), placed by two numbers at once. It shows whether two measurements move together. Do taller people weigh more? Each dot below is one made-up person:

Shift + Enter

The dots form a cloud that slopes upwards: on average, more height comes with more weight, though not for everyone. alpha=0.6 makes the dots a little see-through, so you can spot where many overlap. (Tomorrow you'll measure this "going together" with a number.)

🪟 Two charts side by side

plt.subplots(1, 2) makes a figure with 1 row and 2 columns of charts. It gives back two things: the whole figure (fig) and the separate charts, called axes. You draw on each one with the same commands, called on the axes instead of on plt. Labels are set with set_title, set_xlabel and set_ylabel:

Shift + Enter

fig, (left, right) = ... unpacks the two results into variables in one line, the same way a function can return several values (Day 6).

🧭 Which chart should I use?

Your questionChartMatplotlib
How does something change over time?lineplt.plot
Which category is biggest?bar (or horizontal bar)plt.bar, plt.barh
How are the values spread out? Any odd ones?histogramplt.hist
Do two measurements go together?scatterplt.scatter

In normal Python you can save a chart as a picture file, to put in a report or a presentation: write plt.savefig("sales.png", dpi=150) just before plt.show() (dpi, dots per inch, sets the sharpness). On this page you don't need it: every chart already appears under its box, and you can right-click it to save the picture.

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • x and y of different lengths → ValueError: x and y must have same first dimension.
  • No title or axis labels: the reader has to guess what they are looking at. Add units, like "(°C)".
  • Using a line chart for separate categories (products, cities). Lines suggest a path in time; use bars.
  • On subplots, writing left.title("..."). On axes it's left.set_title("...") (and set_xlabel, set_ylabel).
  • Calling plt.legend() without giving the lines a label=: the legend stays empty.

✅ Recap

  • import matplotlib.pyplot as plt; finish with plt.show().
  • Line plt.plot(x, y), bars plt.bar / plt.barh, histogram plt.hist(values, bins=…), scatter plt.scatter(x, y).
  • Labels: plt.title, plt.xlabel, plt.ylabel, plt.legend() with label= on each line.
  • Style: color=, marker=, linestyle=, alpha=; size: plt.figure(figsize=(w, h)).
  • Side by side: fig, (a, b) = plt.subplots(1, 2), then a.plot(...), a.set_title(...).
  • pandas: df.plot(x=..., y=..., kind=...) and series.plot(kind="bar").
  • Save a chart as a picture: plt.savefig("chart.png").
